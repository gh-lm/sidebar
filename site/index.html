A pinned site, open here

Fourteen tabs, thirteen of which you are not reading.

01

It opens on a search box, and leaves your new tab alone.

Most extensions that offer you a search box take over your new tab page to do it. This one puts it in the panel, and the results appear in the panel too — nothing it does reaches outside that strip down the side.

Six engines to pick from, all of which say they do not build a profile of you: DuckDuckGo, Startpage, Brave, Mojeek, Qwant and Ecosia. Your choice sticks.

02

Pin the site. It opens beside the page, not instead of it.

Press + on the rail and whatever you are looking at becomes an icon. Click the icon and the site loads in the panel — your mail, a chat, the docs you keep checking — while the page you were reading stays exactly where it was.

Right-click an icon to change or remove it. Drag to reorder.

03

A scratchpad that outlives the tab you were on.

The address someone read out. The half-written reply. The number you will need in four minutes. It is in the panel, not in a document you have to find, name and save.

04

Snippets that land where the cursor is.

Click one and the text goes into the box you were typing in, at the cursor. Not onto the clipboard to be pasted later — into the field, where you wanted it.

Right-click to edit. Click away and it saves. Nothing to press.

05

It syncs through your bookmarks. That is the whole trick.

Everything you save is kept as ordinary bookmarks in one folder. Your browser already syncs those — Brave and Vivaldi sync bookmarks even though they sync nothing else about extensions — so your panel arrives on your other machine with nothing to sign into.

It installs with permission for no websites at all.

Most extensions ask to read and change your data on every site you visit, on the day you install them. This one asks about a single site, at the moment you first open it in the panel — and you can take that back whenever you like.
