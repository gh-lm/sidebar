Lightmorphic Sidebar

A panel that stays put while you read.

Pin a site, read it here

Open a site, press + on the rail, and it becomes an icon. Click the icon and the site opens in the panel, beside whatever you are reading — mail, a chat, docs, anything you keep flicking tabs for.

Right-click an icon to change or remove it. Drag to reorder.

It follows you, with no account

Your scratchpad, snippets and pinned sites are kept as ordinary bookmarks, in a folder called Lightmorphic Sidebar in Other Bookmarks. If your browser syncs bookmarks, they travel with them.

No sign-in, no server of ours, nothing sent to us. Delete the folder and the data is gone. Move it wherever you like, but keep its name.

Snippets go where you were typing

Click a snippet and it lands at your cursor in the box you were last in. The first time on a site, your browser asks whether Lightmorphic Sidebar may act there — say yes once and it stops asking.

Say no and the snippet is copied instead, ready to paste. Right-click a snippet to edit it: clicking away saves, Escape leaves it, emptying it deletes it. The mark on the right becomes a bin, then a red tick asking whether you meant it.

It starts with access to nothing

Lightmorphic Sidebar installs with permission for no websites at all. It asks about one site at a time, at the moment you first open it or paste into it, and you can take that back whenever you like from the browser's extension settings.

Showing a site in a panel means dropping the headers that normally stop a site being framed — which is why it has to ask.

Search

The panel opens on a search box. Type and press enter: a web address goes straight there, anything else goes to whichever engine you have picked, and the results appear here in the panel.

The row of letters under the box is the engines: D DuckDuckGo, G Google, I Google Images, B Bing, S Startpage, M Mojeek, Q Qwant. Press one and it searches with that engine straight away, and stays lit so you can see which the box is pointed at. DuckDuckGo uses its own lightweight pages, because the full site does not fit a panel even at phone width — and it is asked for its dark results when the panel is dark, since those pages do not follow the browser themselves. The rest follow your browser's own light or dark setting.

Coming back from a result — the arrow, or the house — leaves what you searched for in the box, selected. Press a different letter and the same words go to a different engine.

Pages open in the panel ask sites for their phone layout, because the panel is about a phone wide and a desktop page in it is unreadable. The bar at the top of a page gives you back, forward, reload, a house that returns to the search page, and a percentage. A page wider than the panel is fitted to it from the inside, so nothing runs off the right and nothing is cropped. The percentage in the bar makes everything smaller again if you want it, and remembers what you chose.

Pages opened here are told they are on a phone, in the request and in the page itself, so a site that decides its layout in JavaScript gives you its phone layout rather than a desktop one that no amount of zooming will fit. A tab you open yourself is never touched by this. The page's scrollbar is replaced with a small blob on the right that fades in as you scroll and can be dragged. Back walks the pages the panel has opened, and stepping back past the first one lands on the search page too. A link followed inside a page is invisible to the panel, so back may skip it.

Lightmorphic Sidebar does not replace your new tab page and does not change anything else in the browser. Everything it does happens in this panel.

Light and dark

The circle on the rail follows your browser to begin with, and shows a sun or a moon depending on what is actually on screen — with a small dot to say the browser is choosing. Click it to hold it light, again for dark, again to hand it back.

Closing it

The chevron at the top of the rail closes the panel, which is what gives the page its full width back. Chrome fixes the panel's width and gives extensions no say over it, so there is no half-open state worth having. The toolbar icon brings it back.

Everything stays in your browser

Your scratchpad, snippets and pinned sites are saved as ordinary bookmarks, in a folder called “Lightmorphic Sidebar” in Other Bookmarks.

That means if your browser syncs bookmarks, they follow you to your other machines — with no account to make and nothing sent to us. There is no server. Delete the folder and the data is gone.

Lightmorphic Sidebar starts with access to no websites, so it has to ask before it can show one in the panel. You can settle that now with one answer, or leave it and be asked about each site as you go — either way it is changeable later, under Information.
