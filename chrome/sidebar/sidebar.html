Pinned sites

Open a site, then press + on the rail. Its address is filled in for you and you can edit it before saving. It becomes an icon on the rail; click the icon to open that site here in the panel, like a small window beside the page you are reading.

Right-click an icon to change its address or remove it. Drag icons to reorder them.

Site access

Sidemorphic starts with access to no websites at all. The first time you open a pinned site here, your browser asks permission for that one site — needed because showing a site in a panel means dropping the headers that normally stop it. Say no and it opens in an ordinary tab instead. You can take access back any time from the browser's extension settings.

Syncing

Your pinned sites, scratchpad and snippets are kept in a bookmarks folder called Sidemorphic, in Other Bookmarks. If your browser syncs bookmarks, they travel with them — no account here, no server of ours. Brave and Vivaldi sync bookmarks but not extension data, which is why they live there. Don't rename the folder, but you can move it.

Snippets

Click a snippet to drop it into the box you were typing in. The first time you do that on a site, your browser asks whether Sidemorphic may act there. Say no and the snippet is still copied, ready to paste yourself. Right-click a snippet to open it out and edit it.

Closing it

The chevron at the top of the rail closes the panel, which is what gives the page its full width back. Chrome fixes the panel's width and gives extensions no control over it, so there is no half-open state worth having — hiding what is inside would leave the page just as squeezed. The toolbar icon brings it back.

Everything stays in your browser

Your scratchpad, snippets and pinned sites are saved as ordinary bookmarks, in a folder called “Sidemorphic” in Other Bookmarks.

That means if your browser syncs bookmarks, they follow you to your other machines — with no account to make and nothing sent to us. There is no server. Delete the folder and the data is gone.

Sidemorphic starts with access to no websites. It asks about one site at a time, when you first open it in the panel.
